## Publicly described methodology distilled for practice

1. Executive communication is a strategy skill, not an accent contest. Structure, delivery,
   audience awareness, cultural decoding, credibility, and impact matter more than ornamental
   vocabulary.
2. Three recurring executive-presence failure modes are excessive apology, excessive praise, and
   excessive context. The public lesson recommends responding to mistakes with action, building
   authentic level-ground relationships, and communicating with senior-level brevity.
3. Managing up starts with understanding a manager's preferred communication method and point of
   view, anticipating questions and roadblocks, and maintaining a predictable update loop. The aim
   is to progress from reactive task-taker to trusted strategic partner.
4. Influence without authority requires stakeholder mapping, strategic alignment, and deliberate
   communication before, during, and after consequential discussions.
5. Crucial-meeting preparation includes identifying the desired outcome, anticipating resistance
   and worst-case scenarios, and preparing a reframing strategy rather than improvising under
   pressure.
6. Meeting leadership covers the whole operating cycle: prepare, facilitate, read the room, handle
   disagreement with grace, recap decisions, assign ownership, and follow through.
7. Visibility should come from useful judgment and proactive communication, not volume. Leaders
   adapt to the audience, ask high-quality questions, and make their contribution easy to evaluate.
